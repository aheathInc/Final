1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This document provides a complete system analysis and design (SA&D) specification for A-health, a proactive digital health platform tailored for African healthcare environments. It is intended to be used directly by software engineers, mobile developers, database administrators, and DevOps engineers to design, build, test, and deploy the system. It translates the business concept described in the A-health pitch material into concrete functional requirements, data models, architecture, APIs, and workflows.

1.2 Project Background

Healthcare delivery across much of Africa suffers from five structural weaknesses: overcrowded physical facilities, a large pool of unemployed/underplaced medical graduates, loss of contact with patients after discharge, poor medication adherence leading to drug resistance, and late diagnosis of serious disease due to limited screening infrastructure. A-health addresses these gaps with a digital-first platform that connects patients to available clinicians via low-bandwidth channels (SMS, USSD, and a smartphone app), automates preventative screening and follow-up, and coordinates emergency transport when needed.

1.3 Objectives of the System

  • Give any patient, regardless of location or device, a way to reach a licensed clinician without physically queueing at a hospital.

  • Create a formal, queue-based system that routes patient demand to underemployed doctors and nurses.

  • Maintain continuous contact with discharged patients through automated, scheduled digital check-ins.

  • Improve medication adherence through reminders and track dosage compliance to reduce antimicrobial resistance.

  • Support early screening/risk triage using structured symptom intake and rules-based/ML risk scoring.

  • Coordinate emergency transport requests and route them to the nearest available responder.

  • Provide administrators and public-health partners with aggregated, anonymized data for outbreak and resource-planning purposes.

1.4 Scope

The scope of Version 1.0 covers four client surfaces (Patient App, Clinician App, Admin/Ops Console, SMS/USSD Gateway Service) backed by a shared set of backend services, described fully in Sections 3–7. Payment/billing, insurance-claims integration, and national health-ID integration are noted as future phases in Section 9 but are not built into V1.

2.2.2 Consultation & Dispatch (FR-CN)

Requirement

FR-CN-01

FR-CN-03

System shall route each CR to the best-matched available clinician based on specialty, language, current load, and geographic proximity.

FR-CN-04

FR-CN-05

Clinician shall be able to accept, decline, or reassign a CR from a live queue view.

FR-CN-06

System shall support real-time text chat, voice call (VoIP or PSTN callback for feature phones), and asynchronous store-and-forward messaging for low-connectivity areas.

FR-CN-07

System shall log a structured consultation note (diagnosis, advice, prescription if any) at the close of each CR, signed by the attending clinician.

FR-CN-08

System shall allow a clinician to refer a patient to a specialist within the network for a second opinion.

ID

Requirement

FR-EM-01

System shall allow a patient (or an attending clinician on their behalf) to trigger an Emergency Transport Request with GPS or manually-entered location.

FR-EM-02

2.2.4 Follow-Up & Adherence (FR-FU)

ID

Requirement

FR-FU-01

System shall auto-generate a Follow-Up Cycle whenever a consultation results in a diagnosis, discharge, or prescription, with a clinician-defined check-in frequency and duration.

FR-FU-02

System shall send scheduled check-in prompts (app push, SMS, or automated voice/USSD) asking the patient to report status against a short structured questionnaire.

FR-FU-03

System shall flag a check-in response as 'Deviation' when reported values fall outside clinician-set thresholds, and create a high-priority review task for the assigned clinician.

FR-FU-04

System shall send medication reminders at prescribed times/frequency and log patient-confirmed adherence (taken / missed / delayed).

FR-FU-05

System shall close a Follow-Up Cycle automatically when recovery criteria (defined by the clinician) are met, or allow manual closure by the clinician.

ID

Requirement

FR-PS-01

FR-PS-03

System shall recommend an action tier based on Risk Score: Self-care guidance / Book routine consultation / Book urgent consultation / Immediate emergency flow.

FR-PS-04

System shall track and remind patients of vaccination schedules (self or dependents).

2.3 Non-Functional Requirements

Category

Requirement

Availability

Core consultation and emergency-dispatch services must maintain 99.9% uptime; SMS/USSD gateway must degrade gracefully if the app backend is unreachable.

Performance

CR routing decision must complete in under 3 seconds at P95; USSD menu responses must render within the telco's session timeout (typically 60–180 seconds).

Scalability

System must horizontally scale to at least 2 million registered patients and 50,000 concurrent active sessions per country deployment.

Low-bandwidth support

All patient-facing flows must be fully usable over 2G/SMS/USSD without a smartphone or data plan.

Security

All PHI must be encrypted at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.2+). Role-based access control (RBAC) enforced at the API layer.

Privacy/Compliance

System must support data-subject access/deletion requests and align with local data-protection law (e.g., Kenya DPA 2019, Nigeria NDPR, POPIA in South Africa) and, for facilities handling international data, HIPAA/GDPR-equivalent controls.

Localization

UI and SMS/USSD content must support multiple languages (e.g., English, Swahili, French, Hausa, Amharic) with per-country configuration.

Offline resilience

Mobile app must cache first-aid content, medication schedule, and last-known consultation notes for offline access.

Auditability

Every clinical decision and PHI access must be traceable to a user, timestamp, and reason.

Interoperability

System must expose HL7 FHIR-compatible export for patient records to support future integration with national EHR/health-ID systems.

2.4 Use Case Diagrams (Textual Description)

The following core use cases summarize primary system interactions. Each is expanded into a full flow in Section 6.

  • UC-1: Patient submits a consultation request and receives a clinician response.

  • UC-2: Patient requests emergency transport and receives first-aid guidance while waiting.

  • UC-3: Clinician completes a consultation and the system auto-generates a follow-up cycle.

  • UC-4: Patient responds to a scheduled follow-up check-in; a deviation triggers clinician review.

  • UC-5: Patient completes a preventative screening questionnaire and receives a risk-based recommendation.

  • UC-6: Admin verifies a new clinician registration.

  • UC-7: Patient interacts with the platform entirely via USSD on a feature phone.

 


 

 

3. System Architecture

3.1 Architectural Style

A-health uses a modular, service-oriented backend behind a single API Gateway, deployed as containerized microservices (or well-bounded modules within a modular monolith for V1, to reduce operational overhead while keeping clean service boundaries for a future split). Clients never call internal services directly.

3.2 High-Level Component Diagram (Textual)

Client Layer:

  • Patient Mobile App (Android-first, iOS second) — React Native or Flutter

  • Clinician App (mobile + responsive web) — React Native / React

  • Admin & Ops Console — React (web only)

  • SMS/USSD Gateway Bridge — server-side integration, no client UI

Edge Layer:

  • API Gateway (routing, auth token validation, rate limiting, request logging)

  • Load Balancer / CDN for static assets and app updates

Application/Service Layer (backend microservices):

  • Identity & Access Service — registration, OTP, login, RBAC, clinician verification

  • Consultation & Dispatch Service — CR lifecycle, triage engine, matching/routing engine

  • Messaging Service — chat, voice-call signaling, push notifications, SMS/USSD outbound

  • Follow-Up & Adherence Service — scheduling engine, check-in processing, deviation detection

  • Screening & Risk Service — questionnaire engine, risk-scoring rules/ML model

  • Emergency & Dispatch Service — transport request matching, live tracking, facility alerts

  • Ratings & Trust Service — ratings, misconduct reports, audit case management

  • Analytics & Reporting Service — aggregation pipeline, outbreak dashboards, admin reports

  • Notification Service — unifies push/SMS/USSD/voice notification dispatch with templating

Data Layer:

  • PostgreSQL (primary relational store — patients, clinicians, consultations, prescriptions)

  • Redis (session cache, real-time queue state, rate limiting, pub/sub for chat presence)

  • Object Storage / S3-compatible bucket (documents, voice notes, verification files)

  • Time-series store (e.g., TimescaleDB extension on Postgres) for vitals/adherence logs and analytics

  • Message Broker (Kafka or RabbitMQ) for async events: CR created, CR escalated, checkup due, deviation flagged

External Integrations:

  • SMS/USSD Aggregator (e.g., Africa's Talking, Twilio) for outbound/inbound SMS and USSD sessions

  • VoIP/PSTN provider for voice consultations and callback-based consults

  • Push Notification service (Firebase Cloud Messaging)

  • Maps/geolocation provider for dispatch distance calculation and ETA

  • Payment gateway (future phase — mobile money, e.g., M-Pesa, MTN MoMo)

3.3 Deployment Architecture

Recommended deployment is a container-orchestrated cloud environment (Kubernetes) split into at least three environments: development, staging, and production, each with isolated databases. Each country/region deployment should be able to run as an isolated tenant to satisfy in-country data-residency requirements common in African data-protection law.

4. Database Design

4.1 Entity-Relationship Overview

The core relational schema centers on Users (Patients, Clinicians, Admins), Consultation Requests, Follow-Up Cycles, and their supporting entities. Relationships are summarized below, followed by full field-level table definitions for each entity.

  • A User (Patient) has many Consultation Requests (1:N).

  • A Consultation Request has one assigned Clinician (N:1), zero-or-one Prescription, and zero-or-one Follow-Up Cycle.

  • A Follow-Up Cycle has many Check-Ins (1:N) and belongs to one Patient and one Clinician.

  • A Prescription has many Medication Items (1:N), each generating many Adherence Logs (1:N).

  • An Emergency Request belongs to one Patient, optionally links to a Consultation Request, and has one assigned Transport Unit.

  • A Screening Response belongs to one Patient and one Questionnaire Template, and produces one Risk Score record.

  • A Rating belongs to one completed Consultation Request and references one Clinician and one Patient.

  • A Facility has many Clinicians (1:N) and many Transport Units (1:N).

4.3 Indexing & Performance Notes

  • GIST/PostGIS index on facilities(location) and transport_units(current_lat,current_lng) for nearest-neighbor dispatch queries.

  • Composite index on consultation_requests(status, urgency_level, created_at) to power the live clinician queue view.

  • Partial index on clinician_profiles(is_available) WHERE verification_status='verified' to speed up matching-engine candidate lookups.

  • check_ins and adherence_logs should be partitioned by month (Timescale hypertable) given high write volume at scale.

  • All PHI-bearing tables (patient_profiles, consultation_notes, prescriptions) should have row-level security policies scoping access to the assigned clinician, the patient themself, and admins with a logged reason.


 

 

5. API Design

5.1 Conventions

  • REST over HTTPS, JSON payloads, base path /api/v1/.

  • Authentication: Bearer JWT (access token 15 min TTL + refresh token 30 days) issued by the Identity Service after OTP or password verification.

  • All list endpoints support pagination via ?page= and ?page_size=, returning { data, meta: { page, page_size, total } }.

  • Errors follow a standard envelope: { error: { code, message, details } } with appropriate HTTP status codes (400, 401, 403, 404, 409, 422, 500).

  • Idempotency-Key header required on all POST endpoints that create clinical or dispatch records, to safely handle retries over unreliable mobile networks.

6. Core Workflows

6.1 UC-1: Standard Consultation Flow

  • 1. Patient submits symptoms via app/SMS/USSD → POST /consultations.

  • 2. Triage Engine (Section 7.1) computes urgency_level and sla_deadline_at.

  • 3. Matching Engine (Section 7.2) ranks available, verified clinicians and pushes the CR to the top candidate's live queue (Redis pub/sub + push notification).

  • 4. Clinician accepts (POST /consultations/{id}/accept) within the SLA window; if not, the Escalation Worker re-ranks and pushes to the next candidate, repeating until accepted or a human dispatcher is alerted.

  • 5. Clinician and patient exchange messages/voice call through the Messaging Service.

  • 6. Clinician closes the CR via POST /consultations/{id}/complete with diagnosis, advice, and optionally a nested prescription and follow_up_cycle payload.

  • 7. System asynchronously creates the Prescription, schedules Adherence reminder jobs, and creates the Follow-Up Cycle with its first Check-In scheduled.

  • 8. Patient receives a rating prompt after a configurable delay (e.g., 1 hour post-completion).

6.2 UC-2: Emergency Transport Flow

  • 1. Patient (or clinician on their behalf) triggers POST /emergency-requests with location and condition_summary.

  • 2. Emergency Dispatch Service queries transport_units via PostGIS nearest-neighbor search filtered by is_available=true.

  • 3. Selected unit is notified (driver app push + SMS fallback); request status becomes dispatched.

  • 4. Patient app/SMS shows live ETA; GET /first-aid-guides is surfaced automatically based on condition_summary's symptom category.

  • 5. Destination facility receives an advance notification with the condition summary via the Notification Service.

  • 6. Driver updates status through en_route → arrived → completed via PATCH /emergency-requests/{id}/status.

6.3 UC-4: Follow-Up Check-In with Deviation

  • 1. A scheduler (cron/queue worker) creates a due Check-In and sends a prompt via the patient's preferred channel.

  • 2. Patient responds via POST /check-ins/{id}/respond with structured answers (e.g., temperature, pain level, wound appearance).

  • 3. Server evaluates responses against the cycle's recovery_criteria thresholds; if any threshold is breached, is_deviation=true and a high-priority task is pushed to the assigned clinician's dashboard plus an SMS/push alert.

  • 4. Clinician reviews the deviation, may escalate to a new Consultation Request, adjust the prescription, or reassure and close the flag.

  • 5. Cycle continues until recovery_criteria are fully met (auto-close) or the clinician manually closes it.

6.4 UC-7: USSD-Only Patient Journey

  • 1. Patient dials the shortcode; aggregator POSTs to /gateway/ussd/session with an empty input (session start).

  • 2. Bridge returns the root menu: 1) New health issue 2) My medications 3) My appointments 4) Emergency.

  • 3. Each subsequent key-press is a new POST to the same endpoint carrying session_id and the input; the bridge looks up session state in Redis and calls the appropriate internal API (e.g., selecting '1' then typing a short symptom code triggers the same POST /consultations call used by the app).

  • 4. Final screen confirms submission and gives a reference code the patient can use to check status by dialing back in or receiving an SMS update.


 

 

7. Key Algorithms

7.1 Triage / Urgency Classification

V1 uses a deterministic, explainable rules engine (not a black-box model) so clinical staff can audit and adjust it. It evaluates structured_symptoms against a configurable rule set stored in the screening_questionnaires-style config table, producing one of: routine, urgent, emergency.

function classifyUrgency(symptoms, patientProfile):

  if symptoms.hasAny(EMERGENCY_FLAGS):        # e.g. chest_pain+breathlessness, severe_bleeding,

      return 'emergency'                      #      loss_of_consciousness, stroke_signs, difficulty_breathing

  score = 0

  for symptom in symptoms:

      score += RULE_WEIGHTS[symptom.code]

  score += AGE_RISK_MODIFIER(patientProfile.age)

  score += COMORBIDITY_MODIFIER(patientProfile.chronic_conditions)

  if score >= URGENT_THRESHOLD:  return 'urgent'

  return 'routine'

EMERGENCY_FLAGS, RULE_WEIGHTS, and thresholds must be editable by Platform Admins via FR-AD-03 without a deployment, and every classification decision is stored with the exact rule/weights version used, for auditability.

7.2 Clinician Matching / Ranking

Given a CR, candidate clinicians are filtered to verification_status='verified', is_available=true, specialty compatible with the CR's inferred category, and language overlap with the patient. Candidates are then ranked by a weighted score:

rank_score = (W1 * proximity_score)

           + (W2 * (1 / (1 + current_load)))

           + (W3 * rating_avg_normalized)

           + (W4 * specialty_match_score)

Default weights (tunable per deployment): W1=0.3, W2=0.3, W3=0.2, W4=0.2. The CR is pushed to the top-ranked candidate first; if not accepted within the SLA window, it is pushed to the next 1–3 ranked candidates simultaneously (broadcast), and if still unaccepted, a human Dispatcher is alerted.

7.3 Risk Scoring (Preventative Screening)

Each screening_questionnaire defines per-answer weights in scoring_rules. The response's total weighted score (0–100) maps to a recommended_action via configurable bands, e.g. 0–29 self_care, 30–59 book_routine, 60–84 book_urgent, 85–100 emergency. As with triage, bands and weights must be admin-configurable and versioned.


 

 

8. Security, Privacy & Compliance Design

8.1 Authentication & Authorization

  • OTP-based login for patients (SMS-delivered, 6-digit, 5-minute expiry, rate-limited to prevent SIM-swap abuse).

  • JWT access tokens (short-lived) + refresh tokens (rotated on use, revocable) for all roles.

  • RBAC enforced centrally at the API Gateway and re-validated at the service layer; permissions are role + resource-ownership based (e.g., a clinician may only read consultation_notes for CRs assigned to them).

  • MFA (TOTP or SMS) required for facility_admin and platform_admin roles.

8.2 Data Protection

  • Encryption at rest: AES-256 for database volumes and object storage; column-level encryption for highly sensitive fields (e.g., chronic_conditions) where feasible.

  • Encryption in transit: TLS 1.2+ enforced on all public endpoints; certificate pinning recommended on mobile apps.

  • PHI access requires a logged reason for administrative roles (FR-AD-04) — no silent admin browsing of patient records.

  • Data residency: each country deployment's primary database and backups remain within that country's cloud region where local law requires it.

8.3 Privacy & Regulatory Alignment

  • Support data-subject access, correction, and deletion requests (right to be forgotten) with a documented SLA.

  • Explicit consent capture at registration for data use in aggregated/anonymized public-health analytics; patients may opt out of non-essential data sharing.

  • Anonymization pipeline for analytics: outbreak/risk-pool dashboards must never expose patient-identifiable fields; k-anonymity threshold (e.g., suppress any regional cell with fewer than 5 cases) enforced in the aggregation service.

8.4 Clinical Safety Controls

  • All triage and risk-scoring outcomes are advisory; the UI must always present an unambiguous path to escalate to a human clinician or emergency services.

  • Every consultation_note must be attributable to a verified, licensed clinician (signed_by_clinician_id is mandatory and validated server-side against verification_status='verified').

  • Deviation alerts and emergency requests are never silently dropped — a dead-letter queue and on-call alerting policy must exist for the Escalation Worker and Emergency Dispatch Service.
