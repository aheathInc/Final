# Patient medication follow-up

THESIS: Let a patient act on a clinician-confirmed medication schedule and understand their own reports and estimated supply without presenting silence as a missed dose.
OWN-WORLD: Incumbent A-Health native Material, petrol/mint/paper, existing ProfileCard gradients and shadows. User-approved identity retained. Code-led extension, no new visual world or comp.
STORY: Home medicines entry opens reminder consent, self-reported adherence for 7/30 days, daily trends, nearby doses with Taken/Skipped(reason)/Snooze, clinician-authored information and patient-reported supply. Whole calendar/SMS preferences and prescriptions remain linked.
FIRST VIEWPORT: App bar and account context, on-device notification consent, period selection and self-reported percentage. Remainder scrolls naturally; low stock and emergency actions must be reachable with large text.
FORM: Android Flutter phone, sw/en/fr, light/dark and native text scaling. Raw clinician text remains untouched. No fabricated pill photography; neutral icon when the clinician has not supplied an image. Quiet muted trend bars support the counts rather than imply clinical verification.

Reminder constraints: explicit notification consent; generic private lock-screen copy; exact timing depends on Android alarm permission/settings. Cached alarms survive app closure and reboot and are cleared at logout/account change. Up to 400 upcoming alarms are cached; the screen exposes their coverage end. Schedule updates need connectivity and app opening. Refills produce a generic notification once per patient-reported stock baseline when low supply is fetched in-app. No background stock monitoring or delivery guarantee. SMS remains provider-dependent; WhatsApp is not configured. Snooze keeps original scheduledAt and does not report medicine taken. New skipped-dose UI requires a reason. Legacy reporting endpoints retain backwards compatibility.

Data: owned patients/authorized guardians only; active clinician-confirmed plans only in metrics. Due denominator excludes future doses. Explicit skipped, delayed, unconfirmed counts remain distinct. Estimated stock subtracts only explicit patient-entered units-per-dose on reported taken doses; unknown units are not inferred from clinical text. No automatic medication changes.
